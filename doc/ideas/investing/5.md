Step 5 — Compute Market
Purpose: detect whether the market is absorbing newly available compute, or whether compute supply is beginning to exceed workload demand.
The underlying hypothesis is:
Efficiency gains > workload growth → required compute falls → excess compute appears → compute prices fall.
Rather than trying to separately estimate efficiency and workload, we observe the final market effect directly.
5.1 — AI Efficiency — future enhancement
Definition:
AI efficiency = the amount of useful AI capability or workload produced per unit of compute/hardware/cost.
Examples could include:
* inference performance per GPU
* tokens/sec per GPU
* AI performance per hardware dollar
* model capability per unit of compute
* inference cost per unit of useful output
We are not tracking this initially.
The reason is that measuring it cleanly across different models, hardware generations and workloads is difficult, while its effect should ultimately appear in the compute market.
Potential future sources:
* MLPerf
* Artificial Analysis
* Epoch AI
* NVIDIA / AMD benchmarks
5.2 — Observed Compute Market — core
Definition:
Observed compute demand = the market-clearing demand for actual GPU compute, observed through compute rental prices and available capacity.
This is the practical proxy for the effect we’re interested in.

Step 5 therefore remains deliberately simple:
5.1 AI efficiency: defined but deferred.
5.2 Compute market: directly measured and included in the dashboard.
The conceptual advantage is that we don’t need to know exactly why compute demand is changing. If efficiency gains are overtaking workload growth, that should eventually manifest as weakening demand for marginal compute capacity.

Rationale for 5.2: Compute marketplaces provide a revealed-demand proxy that does not depend on knowing the workload or compute consumption of proprietary AI models. If increasingly capable/efficient open-source models continue generating sufficient demand, that demand should still manifest through GPU rental markets. Therefore, falling compute prices and rising available capacity can provide evidence of weakening aggregate demand even when proprietary model usage is opaque.

One caveat: Vast.ai represents only a subset of the global compute market, so we’re using it as a market thermometer, not claiming it measures global utilization. The fact that we also have ComputeTape as an independent H100 price series makes the signal more useful.
